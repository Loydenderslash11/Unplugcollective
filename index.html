Statement of Purpose

We Are Not Anti-Technology.
We Are Pro-You.

Somewhere between the first smartphone and today, the internet stopped being a tool we picked up and became an environment we never leave. The average American teenager now spends more than 7 hours per day staring at a screen — not counting schoolwork. That is nearly half of every waking hour handed over to algorithms designed by engineers in Silicon Valley whose singular metric of success is your continued engagement.

This is not an accident. The same behavioral science that powers slot machines — variable reward schedules, infinite scroll, social validation loops — has been deliberately embedded into every platform competing for your attention. Research published in JAMA Pediatrics found that teens who spend more than three hours daily on social media face a significantly elevated risk of depression, anxiety, and sleep disorders. The surgeon general of the United States issued a formal advisory. The evidence is not contested.

The Unplug Collective does not ask you to throw your phone into the ocean. We ask something harder: that you become intentional. One deliberate hour offline each day — reading, moving, creating, talking face-to-face — is enough to begin rewiring your relationship with technology. Neuroscience confirms that even brief, consistent breaks from screens measurably reduce cortisol levels, improve deep-focus capacity, and restore the emotional regulation pathways that compulsive scrolling degrades over time.

You deserve a mind that belongs to you. Join us.

Concept & Rhetorical Strategy

"The 30-Second Disconnect" deploys visual contrast as its central rhetorical engine. The chaotic phase — red-tinted, rapidly blinking, overloaded with notification text and a racing content feed — viscerally recreates the neurological state of compulsive scrolling: fragmented, anxious, relentless.

The abrupt transition to the calm phase — slow, teal-lit, breathing concentric circles — enacts the message rather than merely stating it. The audience does not read that silence feels different; they feel it. This is the rhetorical mode of enargeia: making the abstract vividly present.

The 12-second cycle mirrors the brain's own attention-reset window, creating an experiential parallel between the animation's rhythm and the internal rhythm the campaign advocates for. The piece speaks to pathos through sensory overwhelm and sensory relief, and to logos through the empirical statistics that anchor each phase.

Written Rationales

Rhetorical Analysis

Each campaign element is grounded in deliberate rhetorical strategy. Below are condensed rationales for our four primary pieces. Read full versions on the dedicated rationales page.

01

Slogan Rationale

"Scroll Less, Live More."

The slogan operates on the principle of antithetical parallelism: two imperative clauses, structurally mirrored, that set digital passivity against embodied vitality. The verbs "Scroll" and "Live" are carefully chosen — one is mechanical and mindless, the other is the fullest expression of human agency.

Its brevity is strategic: the slogan must function across all media — a banner, a T-shirt, a spoken chant, a hashtag. At four words, it is impossible to misremember. The comma creates a beat, a pause that is itself a small performance of the message.

Parallelism Antithesis Pathos Concision Imperative Mood
02

Visual Piece I Rationale

"The Digital Cage"

The central metaphor of confinement — the cage — works because it reframes a freely chosen behavior as a loss of freedom. The CSS-rendered phone frame, overlaid with vertical bars that echo prison architecture, forces the viewer to see their device as a structure of containment rather than liberation.

The blue-glow aesthetic is not merely decorative: it deliberately reproduces the quality of light that dominates a teenager's nocturnal visual field, triggering recognition before conscious analysis begins. The flicker of app icons enacts the dopamine loop itself, making the poster a performance of what it critiques.

Metaphor Pathos Visual Irony Ethos (Data) Audience
03

Visual Piece II Rationale

"Recharge Your Life"

The comparative infographic structure privileges logos — the appeal to reason and evidence. By placing two columns side by side within an identical 24-hour frame, the piece makes its argument through juxtaposition rather than assertion. The viewer's eye performs the comparison; the conclusion feels self-generated rather than imposed.

The color grammar (red for the scrolling day, green for the unplugged day) taps into deeply rooted cultural semiotics that require no verbal explanation. The piece also addresses kairos: it speaks to the specific historical moment when teen mental health data has reached a crisis threshold that demands a public response.

Logos Juxtaposition Kairos Color Semiotics Data Rhetoric
04

Multimedia Rationale

"The 30-Second Disconnect"

Animated rhetoric operates at the intersection of time and sensation, two dimensions unavailable to static media. "The 30-Second Disconnect" exploits both: the chaotic phase unfolds over time, building a sense of fatigue that makes the calm phase feel like relief rather than mere contrast.

The piece is addressed to teenagers who have grown up in the attention economy and may be desensitized to verbal warnings about screen use. By using the language of the platform — animation, notification aesthetics, feed design — to subvert those same conventions, the campaign meets its audience inside the rhetorical situation it critiques. This is ethos through medium: speaking the audience's visual dialect earns credibility before the argument begins.
